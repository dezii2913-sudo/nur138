📌 Teacher Focus — Chapter 22 (HTN)
Tap to expand the high-yield callouts for this chapter
ACE inhibitors / ARBs (Ace arbs)
  • ACE-I (-pril): cough/angioedema risk, ↑K⁺, monitor creatinine/renal function.
  • ARBs (-sartan): no ACE cough, still ↑K⁺; avoid in pregnancy.
  • Teaching: change positions slowly; report swelling of face/lips/tongue (angioedema).
CCBs (CCB diltiazem)
  • Diltiazem/verapamil: ↓HR/AV conduction → watch bradycardia, heart block.
  • Monitor BP/HR; hold/notify per parameters; assess dizziness/orthostasis.
HTN basics + nursing considerations
  • Check BP/HR before giving; orthostatic precautions and fall risk teaching.
  • Watch renal labs (ACE/ARB) and potassium shifts (ACE/ARB ↑K⁺; many diuretics ↓K⁺).
Adrenergic meds
  • Know α vs β effects: α1 vasoconstriction ↑BP; β1 ↑HR/contractility; β2 bronchodilation.
  • Watch tachycardia/palpitations; assess contraindications (arrhythmias).
Diuretics
  • Loop/thiazide often ↓K⁺ → cramps/weakness; monitor electrolytes.
  • Teach morning dosing; hydration balance; orthostasis precautions.
Beta blockers (selective vs non-selective)
  • β1 selective (cardioselective) generally safer in asthma/COPD; still use caution.
  • Non-selective β-blockers can trigger bronchospasm (β2 block).
  • Do not stop abruptly (rebound).
📌 Teacher Focus — Chapter 26 (Coagulation)
Tap to expand the high-yield callouts for this chapter
Alteplase (tPA)
  • High bleeding risk: neuro checks + bleeding precautions; avoid unnecessary sticks.
  • Monitor for intracranial bleed signs (sudden headache, neuro changes).
Antiplatelet vs anticoagulant
  • Antiplatelet (e.g., Plavix): platelet aggregation—often arterial clot prevention.
  • Anticoagulant (heparin/warfarin): clotting factors—often venous clots (DVT/PE).
Warfarin / Heparin / LMWH
  • Heparin: monitor aPTT; protamine is antidote; HIT risk → monitor platelets.
  • Warfarin: monitor PT/INR; vitamin K is antidote; many drug/herbal interactions.
  • LMWH: more predictable; renal dosing considerations.
Plavix (clopidogrel)
  • Bleeding precautions; report bruising, black stools, coffee-ground emesis.
  • Avoid new OTC/herbals without provider guidance.
📌 Teacher Focus — Chapter 37 (Respiratory)
Tap to expand the high-yield callouts for this chapter
SABA vs LABA (Beta-2 agonists)
  • SABA (albuterol) = rescue; tremor, tachycardia, ↓K⁺.
  • LABA = controller; not monotherapy in asthma.
Corticosteroids for respiratory
  • ICS: rinse mouth to prevent thrush; monitor hoarseness.
  • Systemic: ↑glucose, infection risk; long-term → bone + vision monitoring.
Leukotriene receptor antagonist (montelukast)
  • Controller, not rescue; watch for psychiatric/mood changes.
MDI + spacers
  • Spacer improves delivery; inhale slowly; hold breath ~10 seconds if able.
  • Order: bronchodilator first, then steroid.
Antihistamines
  • Sedation + anticholinergic effects (dry mouth); safety teaching (falls).
Epinephrine in respiratory
  • Anaphylaxis/severe reactions: priority airway/breathing; expect tachycardia.
