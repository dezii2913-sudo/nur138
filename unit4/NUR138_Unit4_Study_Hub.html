🧬 Chapter 45 — Cell Cycle–Specific Drugs

CHEMO HAS A THIN THERAPEUTIC INDEX — tiny gap between the dose that treats and the dose that harms. That's why almost every patient gets side effects.

What Is Cancer?

Malignant = cancerous, invades tissue, metastasizes. Benign = not cancerous, stays put.

Carcinoma = epithelial (most common). Sarcoma = connective/bone. Leukemia = blood/bone marrow.

Paraneoplastic syndrome = symptoms NOT from the cancer itself — from substances the tumor releases. Cachexia (fatigue, fever, weight loss) is most common. Can be the first sign of cancer.

CHEMO HITS ALL RAPIDLY DIVIDING CELLS — not just cancer. Bone marrow → low blood counts. GI mucosa → nausea/stomatitis. Hair follicles → alopecia.

CCS vs CCNS

CCS (Cell Cycle–Specific) = only works during ONE specific phase of division. Best against fast-growing tumors.

CCNS (Cell Cycle–Nonspecific) = works in ALL phases, even resting cells. Good for slow tumors too.

We use MULTIPLE chemo drugs because we're attacking from all sides — different phases, different mechanisms. Reduces resistance, better kill rate.

Antimetabolites (S Phase)

METHOTREXATE (MTX) — Folic acid antagonist. Also treats rheumatoid arthritis and psoriasis. Major risk = bone marrow suppression (BMS). High doses NEED leucovorin rescue to protect bone marrow.
5-FU (Fluorouracil) — Pyrimidine antagonist. Causes palmar-plantar dysesthesia (hand-foot syndrome) = redness, swelling, pain on palms/soles.
TAMOXIFEN — Selective estrogen receptor modulator (SERM) for breast cancer. Blocks estrogen receptors. HIGH RISK for ovarian + endometrial cancer.

Mitotic Inhibitors (M Phase)

VINCRISTINE — #1 neurotoxin. Intravenous (IV) ONLY. FATAL if given intrathecally. Has less bone marrow suppression than others (which is why it's still used).
Vincristine kills cells during M phase (mitosis) by preventing cell division. It's kept despite neurotoxicity because it doesn't suppress bone marrow as much.

Nadir

NADIR = lowest blood cell counts after chemo. Happens 10–28 DAYS post-treatment. Most dangerous window for infection + bleeding.

SIGNS OF INFECTION: #1 = Fever. BUT severely immunocompromised patients may NOT mount a fever. Watch for ANY change — chills, sore throat, cough, redness, new pain.

Tumor Lysis Syndrome

Chemo kills tons of cells fast → they dump their insides into blood:

↑K⁺ · ↑Phosphate · ↑Uric Acid · ↓Calcium

Dead cancer cells release potassium, phosphate, and uric acid. High phosphate binds calcium → calcium drops. High K⁺ = cardiac risk. High uric acid = kidney damage.

Treatment: IV hydration, diuretics, IV calcium, allopurinol (uric acid), aluminum hydroxide (binds phosphate). Severe → dialysis.

💊 Chapter 46 — Cell Cycle–Nonspecific Drugs

Alkylating Drugs

CISPLATIN — NEPHROTOXIC (damages the kidneys). WHY? Damages kidney tubules. FIX: Aggressive intravenous hydration before, during, and after. Also causes ototoxicity (tinnitus) + peripheral neuropathy (tingling).
Cisplatin's metabolites are excreted by the kidneys and directly damage renal tubular cells. Flooding the kidneys with IV fluid dilutes the drug and protects them.

Cytotoxic Antibiotics

BLEOMYCIN → PULMONARY FIBROSIS. The ONLY one that does NOT cause bone marrow suppression. WHY unique? Its toxicity targets lung tissue instead of bone marrow.
DOXORUBICIN → CARDIOMYOPATHY (heart muscle damage). Monitor with MUGA scans (measures ejection fraction). Protect with DEXRAZOXANE. Has cumulative dose limits. WHY? Generates free radicals that damage heart muscle.

Hormone Therapy

Androgens = male hormones. Estrogens = female hormones.

Female cancers: Block estrogen (tamoxifen, aromatase inhibitors). Giving androgens to female → deep voice, facial hair.

Male cancers (prostate): Block androgens. Giving estrogen to male → gynecomastia, impotence.

BONE CANCER → BRITTLE BONES. Calcium leaks from destroyed bone → HYPERCALCEMIA (normal Ca: 8.5–10.5). Signs: confusion, nausea, arrhythmias.

Extravasation

IV chemo leaks into tissue → PERMANENT damage (nerve, tendon, muscle). May need amputation.

STOP infusion → LEAVE IV in → Aspirate → Call MD → Packs per protocol → Elevate → Document. All hospitals have different guidelines.

Oncologic Emergencies — Know the Signs

Renal: ↓ urine, ↑ blood urea nitrogen (BUN)/creatinine, edema. Liver: Jaundice, ↑ enzymes, right upper quadrant (RUQ) pain. Cardiac: Shortness of breath, edema, arrhythmias, ↓ ejection fraction.

🫁 Chapter 50 — Acid-Controlling Drugs

Stomach Cells & Secretions

CellSecretesWhy It Matters
ParietalHClDrug target. pH 1–4. Stimulated by food, stress, fatty meals
ChiefPepsinogen→ Becomes pepsin in acid → breaks down protein
MucousMucusProtective coat against self-digestion

Also: Intrinsic factor (needed for B12 absorption), Prostaglandins (protect mucosa, prevent proton pump activation), Bicarbonate (buffers acid).

H. PYLORI = 90% duodenal ulcers, 70% gastric ulcers. FIRST-LINE: PPI + clarithromycin + (amoxicillin OR metronidazole) × 10–14 days.

Stress Ulcers in Hospital

ICU patients → stress ulcers within 24 hrs from ↓ blood flow + mucosal ischemia + hypoperfusion/reperfusion injury. PREVENT with H2 blocker or PPI. Stop when leaving ICU.

Antacids

Mechanism of action (MOA): Neutralize existing acid (don't prevent it). Also stimulate mucus, bicarbonate, prostaglandins. Many contain simethicone (anti-gas).

TypeGI EffectRenal Safe?Watch For
AluminumConstipation✅ BEST for renal failureCombine with Mg
MagnesiumDiarrhea❌ AVOID in renalAccumulates to toxic levels
CalciumConstipation❌ AVOID in renalKidney stones + rebound hyperacidity
Sodium bicarb—CautionQuick onset, short acting
HIGH AMOUNTS OF SODIUM BICARBONATE → METABOLIC ALKALOSIS. Blood becomes too alkaline → confusion, muscle twitching, arrhythmias. Also bad for heart failure/hypertension (high sodium).

Drug interactions: Adsorption = antacids physically bind other drugs. Chelation = chemical binding → insoluble. Give other meds 1–2 hrs AFTER antacids.

H2 Blockers

Mechanism of action: Block H2 receptors on parietal cells → ↓ acid. Indications: Gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD), peptic ulcer disease (PUD), erosive esophagitis.

CIMETIDINE → IMPOTENCE + GYNECOMASTIA + inhibits P-450 (tons of drug interactions). Usually REPLACED WITH FAMOTIDINE.
H2 BLOCKERS CAN CAUSE PERNICIOUS ANEMIA — reduced acid impairs B12 absorption over time → B12 deficiency → anemia. Also causes peptic ulcer disease.

Elderly: confusion/disorientation. Don't smoke — decreases effectiveness.

PPIs (-prazole)

Mechanism of action: Irreversibly block proton pump → achlorhydria (no acid at all). Cell must make new enzyme to resume. Most potent acid suppressors.

PPIs: EMPTY STOMACH 30–60 MIN BEFORE FOOD. Food ↓ absorption. DO NOT SMOKE. Long-term → C. diff + osteoporosis/fractures + Mg depletion.

Interactions: Sucralfate delays PPI absorption. Clopidogrel interaction (↓ antiplatelet effect).

Sucralfate (Carafate)

WHY USE IT? Forms a "liquid bandage" over ulcers. Acts LOCALLY not systemically. Also BINDS PHOSPHATE → used in chronic renal failure. Give other drugs 2 HRS BEFORE sucralfate.

💉 Chapter 32 — Diabetes Drugs

Pancreas & Blood Sugar

Pancreas = exocrine (digestive enzymes) + endocrine (hormones). Beta cells → insulin (↓ glucose). Alpha cells → glucagon (↑ glucose). Normal blood glucose: 70–100 mg/dL.

INSULIN: Restores the body's ability to use + store glucose. Stimulates lipogenesis (fat storage), inhibits lipolysis (fat breakdown), PUSHES K⁺ INTO CELLS → used for hyperkalemia (high potassium) treatment.

Type 1 versus Type 2

Diagnosis: Fasting blood glucose >126 mg/dL OR hemoglobin A1C (HbA1C) ≥6.5%. Goal: HbA1C <7%.

Symptoms + WHY: Polyuria (glucose pulls water into urine). Polydipsia (dehydrated). Polyphagia (cells starving). Glycosuria. Weight loss. Fatigue. Blurred vision.

BUBBLY/FOAMY URINE = PROTEINURIA = KIDNEY DAMAGE. Not good.

Type 2 = 90% of cases. Insulin deficiency + resistance (receptors less responsive). Multifactorial. Metabolic syndrome: obesity, ↑ clots, abnormal HDL/LDL cholesterol, hypertension.

Acute complications: Type 1 → diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA). Type 2 → hyperosmolar hyperglycemic syndrome (HHS). Long-term: Macrovascular (myocardial infarction, stroke, peripheral artery disease). Microvascular (retinopathy, neuropathy, nephropathy). Gestational diabetes mellitus → insulin only.

Non-Insulin Drugs (Type 2 Only)

METFORMIN = FIRST-LINE for new onset Type 2 when diet/exercise failed. Hinders glucose production. NO hypoglycemia. Primary adverse effects = gastrointestinal (nausea, diarrhea, bloating). CONTRAINDICATED: renal + hepatic disease (lactic acidosis — rare but lethal). HOLD for contrast dye.

Sulfonylureas (glipizide) = stimulate beta cells. CAUSE hypoglycemia.

Glitazones (pioglitazone) = insulin sensitizers. WORSEN HEART FAILURE.

Alpha-glucosidase inhibitors (acarbose) = take with FIRST BITE. Gas.

GLP-1 agonists (Ozempic) = Black box: thyroid C-cell tumors. Weight loss includes muscle loss with protein loss.

SGLT2 inhibitors (Jardiance) = glucose in urine. FDA: Fournier's gangrene.

Hypoglycemia (<70 mg/dL)

Early: confusion, tremor, sweating, irritability. Late: seizures → coma → death.

Corticosteroids antagonize insulin → ↑ blood glucose. Common exam scenario.

⚡ Cheat Sheet — Antineoplastics (45/46)

Thin therapeutic index = tiny margin between helpful and harmful dose
Chemo hits ALL rapidly dividing cells: bone marrow, GI mucosa, hair follicles

CCS = works in one phase (S phase = antimetabolites, M phase = mitotic inhibitors). CCNS = works in all phases. Combo chemo = attacking from all sides.

MTX → BMS → leucovorin rescue. 5-FU → hand-foot syndrome. Tamoxifen → SERM, ↑ ovarian cancer risk.

Vincristine → neurotoxin, IV ONLY, fatal intrathecal. Bleomycin → lungs, NO BMS. Doxorubicin → heart, MUGA, dexrazoxane. Cisplatin → kidneys, HYDRATE.

Nadir 10–28 days. ANC <500 = severe neutropenia. Fever = #1 sign but not always present.

Tumor lysis: ↑K, ↑Phos, ↑Uric, ↓Ca. Caused by rapid cell death. Tx: hydration, allopurinol.

Bone cancer → brittle bones → hypercalcemia (Ca normal: 8.5–10.5).

CSFs: Epoetin→RBC. Filgrastim→WBC. Oprelvekin→Platelets.

Hormones: Androgen to female → masculine effects. Estrogen to male → gynecomastia/impotence.

Extravasation: Stop → leave IV → aspirate → call MD → protocol → document. Every hospital different.

⚡ Cheat Sheet — Acid Drugs (50)

Parietal = HCl (drug target). Chief = pepsinogen→pepsin. Mucous = protective mucus. Also: intrinsic factor (B12), prostaglandins (protect), bicarbonate (buffer).

H. pylori triple therapy: PPI + clarithromycin + amoxicillin/metronidazole × 10–14 days

Stress ulcers: ICU patients within 24 hrs. Prevent with H2 blocker or PPI. Stop at ICU discharge.

Al = constipation, best for renal. Mg = diarrhea, avoid renal. Ca = constipation + kidney stones. NaHCO₃ = quick onset → metabolic alkalosis if too much.

Antacid interactions: Adsorption + chelation. Give other meds 1–2 hrs AFTER.

Cimetidine → impotence + gynecomastia + P-450 interactions → replaced by FAMOTIDINE

H2 blockers: Can cause pernicious anemia (↓ B12 absorption). Elderly = confusion. Don't smoke.

PPIs: Empty stomach 30–60 min before food. DON'T SMOKE. Long-term = C. diff + osteoporosis.

Sucralfate: "Liquid bandage." Binds phosphate (renal failure use). Other drugs 2 hrs BEFORE.

⚡ Cheat Sheet — Diabetes (32)

Pancreas = exocrine + endocrine. Insulin (↓ BG) + glucagon (↑ BG). Normal BG: 70–100.

Insulin: pushes K⁺ into cells (hyperkalemia tx), lipogenesis, inhibits lipolysis.

Dx: FBG >126 or A1C ≥6.5%. Goal A1C <7%. Bubbly urine = kidney damage.

T2 = 90%, insulin resistance, metabolic syndrome. T1→DKA. T2→HHS.

Rapid (lispro) 5–15 min → FEED NOW. Short (regular) 30–60 min → ONLY IV. Intermediate (NPH) CLOUDY. Long (glargine) no peak, 24 hr, never mix. CLEAR BEFORE CLOUDY.

Sliding scale = reactive, BG swings. Basal-bolus = mimics pancreas, preferred.

METFORMIN = first-line T2 when lifestyle failed. No hypo. GI effects. Lactic acidosis if renal/hepatic. Hold for contrast dye.

GLP-1 → thyroid C-cell tumors + muscle loss. SGLT2 → Fournier's gangrene. Glitazones → ↑ heart failure.

Hypo <70: Conscious→oral glucose. Unconscious→IV D50W or SUBQ glucagon (side-lying). Never oral to unconscious.

Corticosteroids → ↑ BG. Gestational DM → insulin only.
